# Coda di Volpe: the story

*Spoilers from the first line to the last.*

"Coda di Volpe" (fox's tail) is the name of the footpath that climbs from the lagoons of Marinello to Tindari. It is also, more or less, the nickname of the man Lara is after.

## The legend

Tindari takes its name from Tyndareus, father of the Dioscuri. Castor was mortal, Pollux was not. When Castor died, Pollux shared his immortality with him: one day in the underworld, one day among the gods, in turn, for ever.

Greek Tyndaris stood on the headland above the sea. Pliny the Elder writes that half of the town slid into the water. The headland has had other stories since. In a cave in the cliff lived Donna Villa, a witch who lured sailors with her song; the scratches on the rock are said to be hers. Above, today, stands the sanctuary of the Black Madonna, *Nigra sum sed formosa*. Below, the sand spit of Marinello changes shape with every storm.

What the game adds: the sailors of Tyndaris entrusted the twins with two stars of metal, the Star of Castor and the Star of Pollux, which kept the sea in check. When the town fell, the Stars were parted. Whoever sets them together again, on the altar of the drowned temple of the Dioscuri, decides where the water goes.

## The people

- **Lara Croft**, a guest in a house in Via Amodeo, Oliveri, with a Byzantine diary that tells where the lost half of Tyndaris lies.
- **Turi 'a Vulpi**, grave robber, once a tuna fisherman. He wants the Stars to sell them, and he has a gang.
- **Donna Villa**, the keeper the cliff has had since the town fell.
- **The twins**, who are not entirely gone.

## 1. Oliveri - Via Amodeo

Night. The lights go out: a fuse has blown, and it was no accident. Turi's men are in the house and the diary is gone. The front door is barred from outside.

Lara gets the power back with a spare fuse from the cellar, deals with the thieves room by room, and takes a padlock key from the one hiding in the box room. The hatch above it leads to the roof. From up there she sees a boat with no lights pulling away towards Marinello.

Down over the neighbours' roofs, a courtyard gate is shut and the gang's lookout is behind it, with the diary under his arm. A computer in the workshop shows a line of hexadecimal: the numbers of the switches that open the gate. Lara gets the diary back, but its last page is on that boat.

## 2. Marinello - The witch of the lagoons

Dawn on the sand spit: the lagoons, the ruins of the tuna fishery, the cliff, and high in it the dark mouth of Donna Villa's cave. The rope ladder is broken.

The way up is through the water. The old ledger of the fishery says where the crank of the winch is and that two levers lie on the bed of the lake. Lara empties the Mergolo through its sluice, pulls the two levers on the dry bed before the chain stops, finds the Seal of the fishery and presses it into its plate to open the reed bed. With the key of the signalman's house, taken from one of Turi's men, she opens the great sluice: the sea fills the Green Lake up to the witch's ledge.

Inside the cave there are five altars and five silent shells. A fresco with a Latin inscription gives their order. Get it wrong and the spirits wake. Across Donna Villa's pool, on a pedestal, lies the Star of Castor. Taking it brings the roof down. Iron rungs climb out into daylight: Tindari.

## 3. Tindari

Afternoon on the headland. The grave robbers have fenced off the dig and their foreman has taken the keys down to the Greek theatre. Lara takes them from him in the arena.

In the Roman house a mosaic says: *Castor, the mortal, treads the earth. Pollux walks among the gods.* The next room is a whole floor of caps, dark and golden, and only the right twin's tiles hold. Under the basilica a floor has fallen onto iron spikes and a rope hangs from the vault.

The road ends at the sanctuary of the Black Madonna. Something glints behind her. Below is the crypt of the twins, with two statues and a bronze wheel beside the gate, and beyond it a shaft that goes down to the sea caves under the headland. Turi went that way.

## 4. The lost half

The sea caves open onto a Roman street under the sea. At its end, a gate between two empty pedestals: each wants the cap of one of the twins. The caps are in the baths and in the cistern.

Beyond the gate is the temple of the Dioscuri. The cella lies eight metres below, and on the altar are two star-shaped sockets. The Star of Castor in its socket lets the sea in. Taken back, the sea goes. The temple can only be crossed by changing it from one state to the other.

Turi is waiting in the cella with the Star of Pollux. He is faster than his men, he throws fire, and bullets are not the answer: he has to be down there when the Star of Castor goes home. He goes down with the temple he came to rob, and the Star of Pollux floats up by itself.

With both Stars on the altar the vault begins to give way and the spirits of the twins come, Castor's and then Pollux's. The way out opens when the last one is gone. Boulders, bats, a gallery going up.

Air. Light. Dawn over the sand spit of Marinello, and both Stars of the Dioscuri in hand.

## What is history and what is not

True: Tyndaris, founded in 396 BC and named after Tyndareus; Pliny's line about half the town falling into the sea; the cave and the legend of Donna Villa; the Black Madonna; the Roman house and its mosaics; the Greek theatre; the shifting sands of Marinello. Invented for the game: the Stars, the Byzantine diary, the drowned temple, and Turi.
